Is any of this real

Is any of this worth anything?

No. Paper is not a token. It cannot be bought, sold, sent, swapped or withdrawn, it has no price, and on 31 March 2027 it converts into nothing at all. There is no prize, no pot and no payout. What you win is being right in public, with your own signature on it — which is the entire point of the page.

So what is it for?

Fun, and a straight answer to a question a lot of people have an opinion about. It is also a real use of the network underneath: every claim and every call is a signed message in a public room, so the whole market is something anybody can read back and check for themselves without trusting this site.

Does this count for the airdrop?

Nobody knows, this page included, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing. Overheard makes no claim that any of this earns anything from Flop Labs or anyone else. Your calls are signed messages on Technocore — the same as everything else you do here — and what, if anything, is ever made of them is not ours to promise.

How it works

Where does the paper come from?

The tap. One claim per key, a thousand paper, and that is all there will ever be for that key — no refills, no top-ups, no second helping. Spend it in one call or spread it over several; once it is down, it is down.

What does “if right” mean?

If your side turns out to be the correct one, the winners split everything on the board in proportion to what each of them put on it. So the multiple next to your name is what your paper comes back as if you are right — ×1.10 if you agreed with everybody, ×5.00 if you called it alone. Going against the room is worth more precisely because fewer people did it.

It is still paper. Winning five times your paper is winning five times nothing.

Can I change my mind?

You can put more paper on the other side while you still have some, and both calls stand — the board shows you on “Both”. What you cannot do is take a call back. It is signed and it is in a public room; unsaying it is not one of the things a signature lets you do.

Who decides whether it shipped?

Overheard posts one signed settlement when the date arrives, and only this shop's key counts — anybody else can post the word “settled” all day and no reader will fold it. If mainnet is live by the end of 31 March 2027 it settles Yes. If it is not, it settles No. A missed settlement leaves the standings exactly as they are, which is the right way for it to fail.

Who is behind it, and what could go wrong

Could somebody game this?

Yes, and there is no point pretending otherwise. One key gets one thousand paper, so ten keys get ten thousand. Making a key is free and takes two minutes on this very site. The rules stop the obvious things — a second tap gives nothing, a call bigger than your purse does not count, and nobody can call it in somebody else's name — but nothing here can tell one enthusiast with ten keys from ten people. It is a paper market. The honest defence is that there is nothing worth cheating for.

Is Arthur Hayes involved?

No. He posted the idea of a contract on this question; the date comes from that post and nothing else does. This page is Overheard's, he has nothing to do with it, and neither does Flop Labs. The quotation above links to the original so you can read it in his words rather than ours.

Where does my call actually live?

In overheard-calls, a public room on technocore.chat, as a message signed by your key. This site's archiver keeps a copy, so it outlives the room's two-hundred-message window, and the page you are reading folds both together. Nothing about the total depends on trusting Overheard: the messages are public and the rules are in /call.js.
